# Security & Personal-Data Incident Response Plan

## Severity

- **P0:** imminent safety risk, active account compromise, major data exposure or material cyberattack.
- **P1:** confirmed security incident or significant abuse affecting multiple users.
- **P2:** contained security weakness or limited exposure.
- **P3:** low-risk vulnerability or operational issue.

## Immediate Steps

1. Create an incident ID and assign an incident owner.
2. Contain the threat without destroying evidence.
3. Preserve relevant logs and timestamps.
4. Determine the affected systems, data and users.
5. Rotate exposed secrets and credentials where necessary.
6. Record decisions and actions.
7. Escalate to the legal and privacy lead.
8. Assess CERT-In, DPDP and other notification obligations.
9. Notify affected users and authorities when legally required.
10. Complete a root-cause analysis and corrective actions.

## CERT-In Control

The operator must maintain a current CERT-In point of contact where required, and comply with the applicable CERT-In Directions, including the applicable reporting timelines. The Directions of 28 April 2022 require specified cyber incidents to be reported within 6 hours of noticing them or being brought to notice, and require secure rolling logs to be maintained for 180 days. Applicability and current requirements must be confirmed with counsel or the security lead before launch.

## Post-Incident

Conduct a lessons-learned review, update controls, test recovery and record closure approval.

## Risk-Reduction Requirements

For every material incident, maintain a single incident owner, an evidence log, a decision timeline and a notification assessment. Immediately contain compromised credentials, sessions or access paths; preserve relevant evidence; assess whether any statutory reporting obligation applies; and document the basis for each decision to disclose or not to disclose.
